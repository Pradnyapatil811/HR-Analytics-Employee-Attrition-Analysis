📈 Graph-by-Graph Project Objectives & Key Insights for GitHub
1. Attrition By Job Role 
Project Objective: Identify specific operational roles facing the highest turnover risk to prioritize targeted retention initiatives.

Key Insight: Sales Representatives experience the highest proportional churn (~39.8%), followed by Laboratory Technicians and Human Resources personnel, indicating that high-stress or entry-to-mid level operational roles require immediate workload and career-path reviews.

2. Attrition By Education Field
Project Objective: Evaluate whether specific academic backgrounds correlate with higher employee flight risk or external market demand.

Key Insight: Employees with backgrounds in Life Sciences and Medical fields form the largest pool of both total workforce and departures, largely reflecting their overall representation in Research & Development and healthcare sectors.

3. Attrition By Gender
Project Objective: Monitor demographic distribution and ensure gender equity and inclusivity in retention trends.

Key Insight: Displays a balanced gender breakdown among departures (approx. 60% Male vs. 40% Female), showing that attrition is driven more by role and compensation factors than gender bias.

4. Correlation between Years At Company and Attrition 
Project Objective: Analyze how organizational tenure impacts the likelihood of an employee resigning.

Key Insight: Demonstrates a clear negative correlation—attrition is heavily concentrated among newer hires within their first 1–3 years, stabilizing significantly as employees cross the 5-year tenure threshold.

5. Attrition By Business Travel
Project Objective: Assess the impact of travel frequency on work-life balance and employee turnover.

Key Insight: Employees engaging in frequent business travel (Travel_Frequently) exhibit a disproportionately high attrition rate (~24.9%) compared to non-travelers (~8.0%), signaling travel burnout as a key churn driver.

6. Correlation Between Job Level and Avg of Monthly Income
Project Objective: Validate internal compensation structures and equity across hierarchical career progression levels.

Key Insight: Confirms a strong positive linear relationship between job levels and average monthly income, validating that compensation scales properly with seniority.

7. Attrition By Marital Status 
Project Objective: Understand how personal life stages and relationship statuses influence professional stability and turnover.

Key Insight: Single employees exhibit a significantly higher vulnerability to attrition (~25.5%) compared to married (~12.5%) or divorced (~10.1%) colleagues, often due to greater job mobility and fewer familial ties.

8. Attrition By Age Group 
Project Objective: Segment employee turnover across generational brackets and career stages to tailor benefits and engagement programs.

Key Insight: Peak attrition is heavily concentrated in the early-career brackets—specifically 28–37 years and 18–27 years—indicating that young professionals seeking rapid career growth are most likely to leave.
